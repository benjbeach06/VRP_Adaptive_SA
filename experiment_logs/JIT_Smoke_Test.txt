VERDICT: do NOT enable the JIT on this profile. It is 5.4% slower overall.

    jit=OFF  rep1  17,506 proposals/s     rep2  17,209 proposals/s     mean 17,358

    ReorderShortSpanExactly                   33.62   40.79   +21.3%       215,391

THE FINDING: the JIT helps exactly what it should -- the O(k^2) farthest-insertion loops gain
7-10%. Those three operators are 3,567 of 355,077 calls, or 1%. Everything else loses 3-8%, and
the worst regression is the operator holding 61% of all calls.

Arithmetic: the farthest-insertion trio saves about 0.47s; ReorderShortSpanExactly alone loses
about 1.54s.

HYPOTHESIS, NOT MEASURED: ReorderShortSpanExactly is also a numeric loop, so it "should" have
gained. At K=4 its spans are 3 or 4 points and the branch-and-bound finishes in a few iterations,
so the JIT's tracing overhead plausibly exceeds what it can recover. The farthest-insertion
operators run 700-2000us per call and amortize it easily.

TESTABLE PREDICTION: rerun with EXACT_REORDER_MAX_SPAN = 8, where this operator cost 368us per
call and held 48% of the clock. The sign may flip. The K decision may have decided whether the
JIT is worth having.

WIDER POINT: the JIT delivered a real 10% on the expensive operators and bought nothing, because
they are 1% of calls. Any optimization aimed at that end of the roster -- JIT, a C port, or
anything else -- is aimed at the wrong 1%.
