# Retros

One file per retrospective. What happened over a stretch of work, what each of us did well, what we
each got wrong, and what changed in HOW we work as a result.

**A retro is not another kind of technical document.** It records the collaboration, not the
system. `design/README.md` carries the canonical map of the other homes -- `design/`, `planning/`,
`RESULTS.md`, `METHODOLOGY.md` -- and lists this folder alongside them.

## What belongs here

- The shape of a period: what was built, measured, decided, abandoned.
- **Attribution.** Who drove which decision. Corrections that changed direction belong to whoever
  made them.
- What went well, for BOTH of us. A confirmed approach is as reusable as a correction and easier to
  lose, because nothing went wrong to mark it.
- What each of us can learn, ranked by time lost rather than by how wrong it was.
- Workflow changes, which are the main output.
- Which memories were written or updated.

## What does not

**Design rationale.** If a retro explains why a mechanism is shaped a certain way, that paragraph
belongs in `design/` and the retro should link it.

**A defect list.** A retro covers the collaboration, not the code. A bug belongs in its commit
message.

## Convention

Retros are **less formal** than design docs. They may carry numbers and narrative, and they may say
"smoke testing revealed" rather than citing a specific measurement, because they record what we
learned and when rather than what is true now.

Each finished plan in `planning/implemented/` links to the retro covering the period it landed in,
and the retro links back -- see
[planning/implemented/scoring-rework.md](../planning/implemented/scoring-rework.md). That pairing is
what makes a learning history: the plan says what was agreed and how it diverged, and the retro says
what the divergence taught us.
